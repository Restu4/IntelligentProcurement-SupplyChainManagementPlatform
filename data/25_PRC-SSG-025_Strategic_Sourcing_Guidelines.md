## Purpose

This document defines the methodology and governance for strategic sourcing initiatives at PT Nusantara Manufacturing Indonesia, ensuring category strategies are developed using structured analytical frameworks rather than ad hoc negotiation, and that sourcing decisions optimize total value rather than price alone.

## Detailed Content

### 1. Strategic Sourcing Process (7-Step Methodology)
1. **Category Profiling**: Analyze current spend, supply base, and demand patterns for the category.
2. **Market Analysis**: Assess supply market dynamics, including supplier landscape, commodity price drivers, and emerging risks.
3. **Supplier Portfolio Strategy** (Kraljic Matrix): Position the category within a Leverage / Strategic / Bottleneck / Non-Critical quadrant based on supply risk and profit impact.
4. **Sourcing Strategy Development**: Determine optimal approach (single-source, dual-source, multi-source, e-auction, long-term partnership).
5. **RFx Execution**: Conduct RFI/RFQ/RFP per PRC-SOP-001.
6. **Negotiation and Contracting**: Finalize commercial and legal terms per PRC-CMP-005.
7. **Implementation and Performance Management**: Transition to operational management, tracked via PRC-SER-003 and PRC-VPR-004.

### 2. Kraljic Matrix Application
- **Strategic Items** (high profit impact, high supply risk): e.g., specialty flavor compounds, palm oil derivatives — managed via long-term partnerships, dual-sourcing development, and joint innovation programs.
- **Bottleneck Items** (low profit impact, high supply risk): e.g., certain single-source spare parts — managed via securing supply continuity (safety stock, alternate qualification) even at modest cost premium.
- **Leverage Items** (high profit impact, low supply risk): e.g., corrugated cartons, standard commodity chemicals — managed via competitive tendering and volume consolidation to maximize price leverage.
- **Non-Critical Items** (low profit impact, low supply risk): e.g., office supplies, standard MRO consumables — managed via catalog purchasing and process efficiency (minimizing transaction cost rather than unit price negotiation effort).

### 3. Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) Analysis
Strategic sourcing decisions must evaluate Total Cost of Ownership rather than unit price alone, incorporating: unit price, freight and logistics cost, quality/rejection cost, payment terms working capital impact, inventory carrying cost, supplier transition/qualification cost, and risk-adjusted cost of potential supply disruption. TCO models must be documented in the Sourcing Decision Memo for all RFP-level sourcing events (above Rp 750,000,000).

### 4. Make vs. Buy Analysis
For categories where in-house production is technically feasible (e.g., certain packaging conversion processes), Strategic Sourcing conducts periodic Make vs. Buy analysis comparing the fully-loaded cost of internal capability investment against continued external sourcing, presented to the Procurement Steering Committee for categories exceeding Rp 5,000,000,000 in annual addressable spend.

### 5. Dual-Sourcing and Risk Diversification Strategy
For Strategic and Bottleneck category items with single-source dependency (per PRC-SRA-007), Strategic Sourcing develops a phased dual-sourcing roadmap targeting a minimum 70%/30% volume split between primary and secondary suppliers within 18 months of risk identification, balancing supply security against the volume discount benefits of consolidation.

### 6. Category Strategy Review Cycle
Each major spend category (representing more than Rp 50,000,000,000 in annual spend) must have a documented Category Strategy refreshed at minimum every 18 months, or immediately upon a material market shift (e.g., significant commodity price disruption, new regulatory requirement, major supplier consolidation in the market).

### 7. Cross-Functional Sourcing Teams
Strategic sourcing initiatives for categories above Rp 750,000,000 annual spend require a cross-functional team including Procurement, Quality Assurance, Finance, and relevant Plant Operations representatives, to ensure sourcing decisions account for technical, financial, and operational considerations beyond commercial terms alone.

### 8. Negotiation Strategy and BATNA Development
Prior to any major negotiation, the responsible Category Manager must document a Best Alternative to a Negotiated Agreement (BATNA) analysis, identifying realistic fallback options (alternate suppliers, specification changes, insourcing) to ensure negotiation leverage is grounded in credible alternatives rather than positional bargaining alone.

## Rules

1. No RFP-level sourcing event (above Rp 750,000,000) may proceed to supplier award without a documented TCO analysis in the Sourcing Decision Memo.
2. Category Strategies for major spend categories must be refreshed at least every 18 months; lapsed strategies trigger a flag in the next Procurement Audit.
3. Dual-sourcing roadmaps for identified single-source risks (per PRC-SRA-007) must be initiated within 90 days of risk classification and achieve the target volume split within 18 months absent documented extenuating circumstances approved by the Supply Chain Director.
4. Cross-functional sourcing teams are mandatory for categories above Rp 750,000,000; sourcing decisions made without required cross-functional input are subject to review and potential reversal.

## Procedures

1. Strategic Sourcing Unit identifies categories due for strategy review based on the 18-month cycle or risk triggers.
2. Category Profiling and Market Analysis conducted, often supported by external market intelligence subscriptions (e.g., commodity index providers).
3. Kraljic Matrix positioning validated with cross-functional input.
4. Sourcing strategy and TCO framework documented and approved.
5. RFx executed per PRC-SOP-001; negotiation conducted with documented BATNA.
6. Contract executed per PRC-CMP-005; performance management handed to Category Manager for ongoing operational tracking.

## Thresholds

| Item | Requirement |
|---|---|
| TCO analysis mandatory threshold | RFP events above Rp 750,000,000 |
| Category Strategy refresh cycle | Every 18 months (categories >Rp 50,000,000,000 annual spend) |
| Dual-sourcing roadmap initiation | Within 90 days of single-source risk classification |
| Dual-sourcing target achievement | 70%/30% split within 18 months |
| Cross-functional team requirement | Mandatory above Rp 750,000,000 annual category spend |
| Make vs. Buy review threshold | Categories above Rp 5,000,000,000 annual addressable spend |

## Penalties

- Proceeding with an RFP-level award without a documented TCO analysis is a process control violation, subject to Procurement Director review and potential requirement to re-run the evaluation with proper documentation before contract execution.
- Failure to refresh a lapsed Category Strategy within the 18-month cycle is flagged in the Procurement Audit Report (PRC-PAR-009) as a governance gap attributable to the responsible Category Manager.
- Missed dual-sourcing roadmap milestones without Supply Chain Director-approved justification are escalated for direct review at the Procurement Steering Committee.

## Approval Levels

Category Strategy documents: Category Manager prepares, Senior Procurement Manager reviews, Supply Chain Director approves for categories above Rp 150,000,000,000 annual spend. TCO-based sourcing decisions follow standard PRC-PAM-008 approval bands based on total contract value. Make vs. Buy analyses and dual-sourcing roadmap deviations: Supply Chain Director approval required.

## Business Scenarios

**Scenario A**: The Flavors & Fragrances category, currently positioned as "Strategic" in the Kraljic Matrix due to high profit impact and high supply risk (single-source dependency on Global Flavours & Fragrances Pte Ltd, per PRC-SRA-007), triggers a mandatory dual-sourcing roadmap. The Category Manager initiates supplier qualification for an alternate flavor house within the required 90-day window and targets the 70%/30% volume split by Q4 2027, consistent with both this policy and the mitigation plan referenced in the Supplier Risk Assessment.

**Scenario B**: A Make vs. Buy analysis for in-house corrugated carton conversion (addressable spend of Rp 78,000,000,000 annually with PT Karton Pak Nusantara) concludes that the capital investment required (estimated Rp 42,000,000,000 for converting equipment) would not achieve payback within the Company's standard 5-year capital investment hurdle, given uncertain long-term packaging format trends; the Strategic Sourcing Unit recommends continuing external sourcing while monitoring the analysis annually for changing economics.
