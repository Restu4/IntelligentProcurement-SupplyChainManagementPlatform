## Purpose

This policy governs the drafting, negotiation, execution, storage, and renewal of all commercial contracts entered into by PT Nusantara Manufacturing Indonesia with suppliers, vendors, and service providers. It ensures contractual commitments are legally sound, financially controlled, and aligned with Company risk appetite.

## Detailed Content

### 1. Contract Types
- **Framework/Master Supply Agreement (MSA)**: Governs recurring purchases of raw materials or packaging over 12–36 months (e.g., agreement with PT Agro Lestari Nusantara for palm oil derivatives).
- **Service Level Agreement (SLA)**: Defines performance obligations for logistics, cold chain, and facility services (see PRC-SLA-023).
- **One-Time Purchase Contract**: For capital equipment or one-off projects exceeding Rp 750,000,000.
- **Non-Disclosure Agreement (NDA)**: Required before sharing any commercially sensitive information during sourcing.
- **Memorandum of Understanding (MOU)**: Non-binding preliminary agreements used during strategic sourcing exploration.

### 2. Contract Lifecycle Stages
Initiation → Drafting/Template Selection → Legal Review → Risk Assessment → Negotiation → Approval → Execution (digital signature via DocuSign) → Repository Filing (Contract Lifecycle Management system, "CLM") → Performance Monitoring → Renewal/Termination.

### 3. Standard Contract Terms
All Company contracts must include, at minimum: (a) clear scope of supply/services, (b) pricing mechanism and revision clauses, (c) Incoterms and delivery obligations, (d) quality and acceptance criteria, (e) liability and indemnity caps (standard cap: 100% of annual contract value, except for IP infringement and gross negligence which are uncapped), (f) termination for convenience (90-day notice) and termination for cause provisions, (g) force majeure clause, (h) anti-bribery and anti-corruption warranty, (i) audit rights, and (j) governing law (Indonesian law, with arbitration via BANI - Badan Arbitrase Nasional Indonesia for disputes above Rp 1,000,000,000).

### 4. Contract Value Thresholds and Review
Contracts are categorized by value for review intensity purposes:
- Below Rp 150,000,000: Standard template, Procurement-led review, Legal review optional.
- Rp 150,000,001 – Rp 750,000,000: Legal review mandatory, redlines tracked in CLM.
- Above Rp 750,000,000: Full Legal and Risk Management review, mandatory Compliance & Ethics Office screening of counterparty, and Finance review of payment terms impact on working capital.
- Above Rp 5,000,000,000: Additional Board Risk Committee notification required prior to execution.

### 5. Contract Renewal Management
The CLM system generates automated renewal alerts at 180, 90, and 30 days before contract expiry. Category Managers must initiate renewal review (incorporating latest Supplier Evaluation Report) no later than 120 days before expiry to avoid lapses in supply continuity. Auto-renewal clauses are discouraged and require explicit Legal approval if used.

### 6. Amendment Management
Any amendment altering price, scope, term, or liability allocation must follow the same approval pathway as the original contract value band. Side letters or informal email agreements that alter contractual obligations are not legally recognized by the Company and are prohibited.

### 7. Contract Repository & Retention
All executed contracts must be uploaded to the CLM system within 5 working days of signature. Original physical copies (where applicable) are retained by Legal & Contracts for a minimum of 10 years post-expiry, per Indonesian tax and corporate record-keeping requirements (UU No. 8/1997 on Company Documents).

## Rules

1. No contract is valid or binding on the Company unless signed by an authorized signatory per the Corporate Signing Authority Matrix.
2. Procurement personnel are prohibited from signing contracts on behalf of the Company unless explicitly delegated signing authority in writing by the Board.
3. All contracts with related parties (as defined in the Procurement Ethics Policy, PRC-PEP-011) require disclosure to the Compliance & Ethics Office prior to execution.
4. Contracts must not be backdated under any circumstance.
5. Exclusivity or single-source clauses exceeding 24 months require Procurement Director and Legal joint sign-off.

## Procedures

1. Category Manager initiates contract request in CLM with commercial terms summary.
2. Legal selects appropriate template or drafts bespoke agreement.
3. Risk Management screens counterparty (financial health, litigation history, sanctions list).
4. Negotiation conducted jointly by Procurement (commercial terms) and Legal (legal terms).
5. Final draft routed for approval per value threshold.
6. Execution via DocuSign with dual signatory verification for contracts above Rp 750,000,000.
7. Contract filed in CLM; renewal alert dates configured.
8. Performance monitored against contract KPIs throughout term.

## Penalties

- Execution of an unauthorized contract by an employee without signing authority renders the employee personally liable for resulting losses and subject to termination.
- Use of undisclosed side letters to alter contractual terms constitutes gross misconduct and is grounds for immediate dismissal and potential legal action against the counterparty for breach.
- Failure to file executed contracts in CLM within the prescribed period results in a compliance non-conformance recorded against the responsible Category Manager's performance record.

## Approval Levels

Contract approval mirrors the Procurement Approval Matrix (PRC-PAM-008) value bands, with the addition that Legal & Contracts sign-off is mandatory for all contracts above Rp 150,000,000 regardless of Procurement-level approval.

## Business Scenarios

**Scenario A**: PT NMI intends to renew its 36-month Master Supply Agreement with PT Agro Lestari Nusantara (annual value Rp 410,000,000,000) for crude palm oil derivatives. Given the value exceeds Rp 5,000,000,000, the renewal requires Board Risk Committee notification in addition to full Legal review, Enhanced Due Diligence, and CFO/Procurement Director approval before the new agreement can be executed.

**Scenario B**: A Category Manager receives an email from PT Maju Bersama Chemical proposing a 5% price reduction in exchange for extending payment terms from Net 45 to Net 60 days, to be confirmed "informally" by email rather than a contract amendment. Per Rule 4 and the side-letter prohibition, this change cannot take effect until a formal contract amendment is drafted, legally reviewed, and approved through the standard amendment pathway.
