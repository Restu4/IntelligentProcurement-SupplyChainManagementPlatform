## Purpose

This report presents the findings of the FY2025 annual internal audit of the Procurement function across PT NMI's Cikarang, Surabaya, and Medan operations, assessing compliance with PRC-SOP-001, PRC-POG-002, PRC-PAM-008, and PRC-PCP-006. The audit covered total procurement spend of Rp 3.74 trillion across 14,820 purchase orders.

## Detailed Content

### 1. Audit Scope and Methodology
The audit team sampled 480 transactions (3.2% of total PO volume, weighted toward high-value and high-risk categories) using a risk-based sampling methodology. Testing covered: approval matrix compliance, three-way match integrity, supplier master data accuracy, contract compliance, and conflict-of-interest disclosures. Fieldwork was conducted between 15 November 2025 and 20 January 2026.

### 2. Overall Audit Opinion
The Procurement function received an overall rating of **"Satisfactory with Improvement Areas."** Core controls (PO approval routing, three-way match, KYS screening) were found operating effectively in the majority of tested transactions. However, five findings were identified requiring management action, two of which are classified High Priority.

### 3. Finding 1 (High Priority): PO Splitting at Medan Plant
Audit identified 7 instances at the Medan facility where a single procurement requirement for maintenance spare parts (totaling Rp 612,000,000 across the 7 instances) was split into multiple POs each below Rp 150,000,000, apparently to avoid Senior Procurement Manager-level approval. This is a direct violation of PRC-POG-002 Rule 2 and PRC-PAM-008 Rule 3. **Root cause**: Plant Procurement Officer cited "urgency" as justification, but no emergency procurement documentation was filed per PRC-SOP-001 Section 7. **Management Action**: Formal disciplinary process initiated against the responsible officer; mandatory refresher training for all Medan plant procurement staff; system control enhancement to flag multiple POs to the same supplier within a 7-day window for the same cost center.

### 4. Finding 2 (High Priority): Expired Compliance Documentation Not Flagged
Testing of 60 active suppliers found that 9 suppliers (15%) had expired Halal certification, ISO certification, or BPOM registration documents in the Vendor Master that had not triggered any system alert or sourcing restriction, contrary to PRC-PCP-006 Section 2. **Root cause**: The automated document expiry alert function in SAP Ariba was not configured for all document types during the 2024 system migration. **Management Action**: IT Procurement Systems team to reconfigure expiry alerts for all compliance document types by 30 April 2026; immediate manual re-verification of all 60 sampled suppliers completed during audit fieldwork.

### 5. Finding 3 (Medium Priority): Incomplete Sourcing Decision Memos
22% of sampled RFQ/RFP transactions above Rp 150,000,000 lacked a complete Sourcing Decision Memo justifying supplier selection, particularly where the lowest-priced bidder was not selected. **Management Action**: Mandatory Sourcing Decision Memo template enforcement via system control (cannot submit PR for approval without attached memo) to be implemented by Q2 2026.

### 6. Finding 4 (Medium Priority): Gift Disclosure Register Underutilization
Cross-referencing expense reports and supplier entertainment claims against the Gift Disclosure Register suggested at least 4 instances where supplier-funded business meals likely exceeded the Rp 2,000,000 pre-approval threshold without corresponding disclosure entries. **Management Action**: Compliance & Ethics Office to conduct targeted awareness campaign and spot-check expense claims quarterly.

### 7. Finding 5 (Low Priority): Blanket PO Quarterly Review Lapses
Of 18 active Blanket POs reviewed, 5 had not undergone the mandatory quarterly competitiveness review required under PRC-POG-002 Section 7 for at least two consecutive quarters. **Management Action**: Category Managers to complete overdue reviews by end of Q1 2026; calendar reminders added to SRM system.

## Rules

1. All High Priority findings must be remediated within 90 days of report issuance, with progress reported monthly to the Audit Committee.
2. Medium Priority findings require a remediation plan within 30 days and completion within 180 days.
3. Repeat findings from a prior audit cycle that remain unremediated are automatically escalated to High Priority regardless of original classification.
4. Internal Audit retains the right to perform unannounced follow-up testing on any finding.

## Procedures

1. Annual audit plan approved by Audit Committee at start of fiscal year.
2. Risk-based sample selected using transaction data analytics (SAP GRC tool).
3. Fieldwork conducted with process walkthroughs and document testing.
4. Draft findings shared with Procurement and Compliance management for factual validation (10 working days).
5. Final report issued to Audit Committee with management action plans and owners.
6. Quarterly remediation tracking until all findings formally closed by Internal Audit.

## Penalties

- Confirmed PO splitting (Finding 1) resulted in formal written warning to the responsible Plant Procurement Officer and a performance improvement plan; a second confirmed instance Company-wide within 24 months would result in termination per HR Disciplinary Policy.
- Department heads failing to close High Priority findings within 90 days are required to present a cause analysis directly to the Audit Committee and may face a negative notation in their annual performance review.

## Approval Levels

This report and all findings are approved by the Audit Committee. Management action plans require sign-off from the relevant function head (Procurement Director for Findings 1, 3, 5; Chief Compliance Officer for Findings 2, 4) and progress is co-reported by Internal Audit and the action owner.

## Business Scenarios

**Scenario A**: Following Finding 1, the Medan Plant Procurement Officer requests a Rp 580,000,000 spare parts order three months later. Under the newly enhanced system control, the system automatically flags the cumulative spend to the same supplier within the 7-day window, forcing consolidation into a single PR requiring Senior Procurement Manager approval rather than allowing a repeat split.

**Scenario B**: A follow-up Internal Audit spot-check in August 2026 finds that 2 of the 9 suppliers identified in Finding 2 still have expired BPOM registrations due to a delay in IT system reconfiguration. Per Rule 3, this unremediated repeat issue is automatically escalated to High Priority status in the next audit cycle, requiring direct Chief Compliance Officer accountability to the Audit Committee.
