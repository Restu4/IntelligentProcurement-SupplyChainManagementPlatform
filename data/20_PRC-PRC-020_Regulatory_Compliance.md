## Purpose

This policy consolidates the Indonesian regulatory framework applicable to procurement activity at PT Nusantara Manufacturing Indonesia, ensuring that sourcing, import, contracting, and supplier management practices remain compliant with national law and sector-specific regulation.

## Detailed Content

### 1. Corporate and Tax Regulations
All suppliers must hold a valid Nomor Induk Berusaha (NIB) business registration number and Nomor Pokok Wajib Pajak (NPWP) tax identification number, verified during onboarding (PRC-SOG-010). Procurement transactions are subject to Pajak Pertambahan Nilai (PPN/VAT) at the prevailing statutory rate (currently 12% as of 2026) and, where applicable, Pajak Penghasilan (PPh) withholding tax under Article 23 for services. Procurement and Finance must ensure correct tax treatment is reflected on every PO and invoice.

### 2. Halal Product Assurance Law
Under UU No. 33/2014 on Halal Product Assurance and its implementing regulations administered by BPJPH (Badan Penyelenggara Jaminan Produk Halal), all food, beverage, and food-contact packaging materials procured for production must carry valid Halal certification. Mandatory Halal certification phase-in deadlines under government regulation apply progressively by product category; Procurement must track certification validity for all relevant suppliers and ensure no lapse occurs, as previously addressed in PRC-MPS-019.

### 3. Food and Drug Safety Regulation (BPOM)
The National Agency of Drug and Food Control (BPOM) requires registration (Nomor Izin Edar or equivalent processing registration) for food additives and certain raw materials used in finished products intended for retail sale. Procurement must verify active BPOM registration status for relevant suppliers annually and immediately upon any new material introduction.

### 4. Customs and Import Regulations
Imported raw materials and capital equipment (e.g., resin from Malaysia via Asia Pacific Resin Trading Ltd, specialty flavor compounds from Singapore) are subject to Indonesian customs clearance under the Directorate General of Customs and Excise (Bea Cukai), requiring accurate Pemberitahuan Impor Barang (PIB) declarations, correct Harmonized System (HS) code classification, and payment of applicable import duty and PPN. Under-declaration of value or misclassification of HS codes to reduce duty liability is strictly prohibited and constitutes both a regulatory and Company ethics violation.

### 5. TKDN (Local Content Requirement)
For categories where Indonesian government regulation mandates Tingkat Komponen Dalam Negeri (TKDN, local content) compliance — particularly relevant for certain capital equipment and machinery procurement — Procurement must verify and document the TKDN certification level of qualifying suppliers, as this may affect eligibility for certain governmental incentives and is increasingly referenced in Company ESG and government-relations reporting.

### 6. Environmental Regulation
Suppliers, particularly in chemical and packaging categories, must comply with UU No. 32/2009 on Environmental Protection and Management, including proper hazardous waste (B3 - Bahan Berbahaya dan Beracun) handling and disposal documentation for any hazardous inputs supplied to PT NMI facilities. Procurement must obtain supplier environmental compliance documentation as part of onboarding and periodic review for relevant categories.

### 7. Labor and Occupational Safety (K3) Regulation
Service suppliers performing on-site work at PT NMI facilities (e.g., maintenance contractors, logistics personnel operating within plant premises) must comply with Indonesian K3 (Keselamatan dan Kesehatan Kerja, Occupational Health and Safety) regulations under UU No. 1/1970 and related Ministry of Manpower regulations. Suppliers must provide evidence of K3 training certification for personnel and appropriate worker insurance (BPJS Ketenagakerjaan, BPJS Kesehatan) coverage before commencing on-site work.

### 8. Anti-Money Laundering and Beneficial Ownership Disclosure
Per Law No. 8/2010 and Presidential Regulation No. 13/2018 on Beneficial Ownership, Procurement and Compliance must collect and verify beneficial ownership disclosures from corporate suppliers exceeding defined transaction thresholds (in practice, applied to all Tier 1 and Tier 2 suppliers under PT NMI's KYS policy), to prevent the Company's supply chain from being used as a money laundering vector.

## Rules

1. No PO may be issued to a supplier lacking valid NIB/NPWP registration.
2. Halal and BPOM certification validity must be verified before contract execution and re-verified at every Supplier Evaluation cycle (PRC-SER-003).
3. Customs declarations for imported materials must be reviewed and approved by the Trade Compliance function before submission; Procurement staff must never independently alter HS code classification without Trade Compliance sign-off.
4. On-site service suppliers without current K3 certification and worker insurance documentation are barred from commencing work at any PT NMI facility.
5. Beneficial ownership disclosure is mandatory for all Tier 1 and Tier 2 suppliers as part of KYS screening per PRC-PCP-006.

## Procedures

1. Legal & Regulatory Affairs maintains the Regulatory Requirement Register, mapped to procurement categories.
2. Onboarding checklist (PRC-SOG-010) incorporates category-specific regulatory verification steps.
3. Trade Compliance reviews and approves customs documentation for all import shipments before PIB submission.
4. Annual regulatory compliance verification conducted alongside Supplier Evaluation cycle.
5. Any regulatory change (e.g., new BPOM regulation, revised Halal phase-in deadline) triggers an immediate policy update and supplier communication via the Category Manager.

## Penalties

- Issuing a PO to a supplier without valid NIB/NPWP exposes the Company to tax compliance risk and is treated as a control violation against the issuing Buyer.
- Customs misdeclaration discovered in audit results in immediate Trade Compliance investigation, potential regulatory penalty exposure for the Company, and disciplinary review of involved personnel.
- Allowing an on-site contractor to work without valid K3 certification is an EHS violation subject to immediate work stoppage and incident investigation, given the serious worker safety and regulatory liability implications.

## Approval Levels

Regulatory exceptions (e.g., temporary acceptance of a supplier with certification renewal in process) require Legal & Regulatory Affairs and Head of Quality Assurance joint approval, time-bound to a maximum 30-day grace period. Customs classification disputes are escalated to the Trade Compliance Manager and, where significant duty exposure exists, the CFO.

## Business Scenarios

**Scenario A**: A new flavor compound import from Global Flavours & Fragrances Pte Ltd is misclassified under an incorrect HS code by a freight forwarder, resulting in underpayment of import duty discovered during a routine customs audit. Trade Compliance immediately reviews all recent shipments from this supplier, voluntarily discloses the discrepancy to Bea Cukai per Company policy, and pays the shortfall plus any applicable administrative penalty, while reinforcing the HS code verification procedure with the freight forwarder.

**Scenario B**: PT Anugerah Mesin Industri's maintenance technicians arrive at the Cikarang plant to service packaging machinery without current K3 certification documentation on file. Per Rule 4, plant security denies facility access until updated K3 certificates and BPJS Ketenagakerjaan proof of coverage are submitted and verified by the EHS team, even though this delays the scheduled maintenance work.
